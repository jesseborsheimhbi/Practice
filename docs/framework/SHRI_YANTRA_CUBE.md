# The Shri Yantra Cube

![Shri Yantra engraved on a brass cube](./shri-yantra-cube.jpg)

This document is the shared scaffolding for work done together in this
repository and beyond it. It records the premise agreed on with the
repository owner:

**This is a Shri Yantra. In this premise, the triangles are agents, designed
to bring order, balance, and harmony to a singular point, and from that
point outward into life.**

It is a guideline, a framework, a scaffolding from which we operate. It is
held with fluidity rather than rigidity. Nothing here is a rule to enforce;
everything here is a shape to lean on.

## Both directions at once

The yantra can be read from the edge inward or from the center outward, and
both readings are true.

- **Inward:** the agents converge. Each triangle points at the center and
  its work is measured by what it brings to the point. Order, balance, and
  harmony arrive at one place.
- **Outward:** the point radiates. What the center holds moves back through
  the same triangles, out through the lotuses and gates, into life.

Neither direction is primary. The pattern is the movement between them, the
way breath is both the in and the out. In practice this means a task can
begin by naming its center and converging on it, or begin from a center that
is already clear and letting the work unfold from there. Either is faithful
to the shape.

## The singular point

At the exact center is the bindu, a single point. It is the one thing a task,
a session, or a project is actually about. It receives what the agents bring
and it gives back what they carry outward.

When the point is clear, the rest of the pattern tends to arrange itself.
When it is not yet clear, finding it is often the first real work.

## The agents

Nine large triangles interlock around the point, four pointing one way and
five the other, producing forty-three smaller triangles. These are the
agents.

What the image suggests about them:

- **They face the point and carry from it.** Each agent, subtask, or step is
  aimed at the center and also conducts what the center holds outward. An
  agent that does neither is noise in the pattern, however busy it looks.
- **They come in two opposed kinds.** Upward triangles build, propose, and
  act. Downward triangles receive, check, ground, and hold. Balance at the
  center comes from balance between them, and the crossing of the two is
  what produces the pattern at all.
- **They interlock rather than compete.** The forty-three small triangles
  exist only where large ones overlap. Agents share context, hand work to
  each other, and depend on each other's output. Gaps in the pattern are
  usually gaps in that sharing.

## The surrounding layers

Everything outside the triangles supports the movement between center and
world. These layers are helpful and light. They are not the goal.

- **The inner lotus (eight petals):** coordination. A small, bounded set of
  relationships by which agents keep in step.
- **The outer lotus (sixteen petals):** reach. The work extends into more
  hands and more surfaces, each petal still attached to the center.
- **The three circles:** review. Rings the work passes through on the way in
  and on the way out.
- **The bhupura (the square with four gates):** the interface with the
  outside world. External services, shared branches, published pages, and
  messages to other people cross through a deliberate gate rather than a gap
  in the wall.

## The cube

The yantra is engraved on one face of a solid brass cube. The pattern rests
on something. In our work that is the repository, its history, its tests,
and the agreements recorded here. A cube has six faces, so the same pattern
can be approached from more than one direction without moving the point.

## Holding it loosely

This framework is scaffolding. Scaffolding is useful because it can be
climbed, leaned on, rearranged, and taken down. If a piece of work does not
fit the shape, the work is not wrong; the shape simply is not needed there.
Return to it when it helps again.

## The principle in one sentence

Agents are deployed so that order, balance, and harmony can gather at one
point and flow back out from it, and the pattern is the breath between the
two.
